## Risk Identification

**Risk = Threat x Vulnerability x Impact**

First step: identify assets, threats, and vulnerabilities that could affect them.

---

## Risk Assessment Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ad hoc** | Triggered by a specific event (new system, incident) |
| **Recurring** | Scheduled periodic assessment (annual, quarterly) |
| **One-time** | For a specific project or acquisition |
| **Continuous** | Real-time risk monitoring integrated into operations |

---

## Risk Analysis

### Qualitative

Subjective assessment: Low / Medium / High / Critical
Uses expert judgment. Fast but imprecise.

### Quantitative

Numerical values. More precise but requires extensive data.

| Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AV** (Asset Value) | What is the value of the asset? |
| **EF** (Exposure Factor) | % of asset value lost if the threat materializes |
| **SLE = AV x EF** | Single Loss Expectancy - cost of one incident |
| **ARO** (Annualized Rate of Occurrence) | How many times per year does this occur? |
| **ALE = SLE x ARO** | Annualized Loss Expectancy - expected annual cost |

**Concrete example:**
- Server worth $100,000 (AV)
- Ransomware destroys 80% of data (EF = 0.8)
- SLE = $80,000
- Ransomware strikes every 2 years (ARO = 0.5)
- ALE = $80,000 x 0.5 = **$40,000/year**

> If a security control costs less than the ALE, it is **financially justified**.

## Risk Register

Documents identified risks for tracking.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Risk description** | What is the threat? |
| **Key risk indicators** | Metrics signaling the risk is materializing |
| **Risk owner** | Who is responsible for managing this risk? |
| **Risk threshold** | Level triggering action |
| **Current status** | Active, mitigated, accepted, transferred |

---

## Risk Appetite vs. Risk Tolerance

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Risk appetite** | Level of risk the organization is *willing* to accept strategically |
| **Risk tolerance** | The *acceptable deviation* from appetite - operational limits |

| Appetite type | Characteristic |
|---|---|
| **Expansionary** | Accepts more risk for competitive advantage (startup, fintech) |
| **Conservative** | Minimizes risk - prioritizes safety (banking, healthcare, defense) |
| **Neutral** | Balanced approach |

---

## Risk Management Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Mitigate** | Implement controls to reduce the risk |
| **Transfer** | Cyber insurance, cloud, outsource to MSP/MSSP |
| **Avoid** | Stop the activity that creates the risk |
| **Accept** | Document and accept the risk - with formal justification |
| **Exemption** | Permanent exclusion from a control |
| **Exception** | Temporary deviation from a control |

---

## Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

Identifies critical business functions and the impact of a disruption.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **RTO** | Maximum acceptable system downtime |
| **RPO** | Maximum acceptable data loss |
| **MTTR** | Mean time to repair after a failure |
| **MTBF** | Mean time between failures (reliability indicator) |

## Practice Questions

**Q: An organization calculates that a ransomware incident would cost $200,000, with a 20% probability per year. A control costing $30,000/year would reduce the probability to 5%. Is this control financially justified?**
- Current ALE = $200,000 x 0.20 = $40,000/year
- ALE with control = $200,000 x 0.05 = $10,000/year
- Savings = $30,000/year - control costs exactly what the savings generate
Answer: **Barely justified** (balance between cost and benefit).

**Q: What is the difference between Risk Appetite and Risk Tolerance?**
Answer: **Risk appetite** = level of risk strategically and voluntarily accepted. **Risk tolerance** = acceptable operational deviation from that appetite (the margin).
