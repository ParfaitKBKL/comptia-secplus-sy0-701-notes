# 1.1 - Compare and Contrast Various Types of Security Controls

**Official CompTIA SY0-701 objective:** 1.1 - Compare and contrast various types of security controls.
**Recommended resource:** Professor Messer SY0-701 - "Security Controls" (Domain 1, Section 1.1)

---

## Control Categories

Controls are organized by **who implements them** and **where they operate**.

| Category | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** (Logical) | Implemented by computer systems and technology. Executed by machines, not people. | Firewall, antivirus, encryption, ACL, IDS/IPS, MFA |
| **Managerial** (Administrative) | Documented in written policies, focused on risk reduction. Defined by management. | Risk assessments, security policies, background checks, data classification |
| **Operational** | Day-to-day organizational procedures, implemented and executed primarily by people (not systems). | Security training, incident drills, badge checks by staff, security rounds |
| **Physical** | Tangible barriers and deterrents designed to protect physical assets. | Fences, locks, CCTV, bollards, access vestibules |

> **Common exam trap:** A "policy" is a Managerial control, not Operational. Operational = *people executing* the policy on a daily basis.

---

## Control Types

Controls are organized by **what they do** in relation to a threat.

| Type | Goal | When it acts | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Stop the incident from happening | Before | Firewall rules, MFA, locked doors, encryption |
| **Deterrent** | Discourage the attacker (psychologically) | Before | Warning signs, visible cameras, uniformed security guard |
| **Detective** | Identify that something happened | During / After | IDS, SIEM alerts, audit logs, CCTV recording, vulnerability scanning |
| **Corrective** | Fix damage after the incident | After | Patch deployment, restore from backup, activate DRP |
| **Compensating** | Alternative when the primary control is not feasible | During | Network isolation for an unpatched legacy system, MFA replacing a broken access control |
| **Directive** | Tell people what to do | Before (policy-driven) | AUP, mandatory training, documented procedures |

### Key Distinctions

| Common confusion | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Preventive vs. Deterrent** | A fence that *physically blocks* = Preventive. A "Video Monitored" sign that *discourages* = Deterrent. |
| **Corrective vs. Compensating** | Corrective *fixes* the problem (patch, restore). Compensating *substitutes* when fixing is not possible (legacy system isolation). |
| **Detective vs. Preventive** | IDS = Detective (detects and alerts). IPS = Preventive (blocks traffic). |
| **Directive vs. Managerial** | Directive = tells people what to do (AUP, training). Managerial = decides overall policy (risk assessment). |

## Control Combinations

Controls are often layered (defense-in-depth). A single scenario can involve multiple categories and types.

**Example - Office building access:**

| Control | Category | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Locked door | Physical | Preventive |
| CCTV cameras at entry | Physical | Deterrent |
| Badge reader (logs entries) | Technical | Detective |
| "Challenge all tailgaters" policy | Operational | Directive |
| Physical security awareness training | Operational | Preventive |
| Emergency evacuation plan | Managerial | Corrective |

## Quick Reference Matrix

| | Technical | Managerial | Operational | Physical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Firewall, MFA, encryption | Access policy, background checks | Security training | Fence, lock, bollard |
| **Deterrent** | Login warning banner | NDAs, disciplinary policies | Security guard presence | "Monitored" sign |
| **Detective** | SIEM, IDS, logs | Audit reviews, risk reports | Log review process | Motion sensor, CCTV |
| **Corrective** | Patch management | Incident response plan | Restore from backup | Repair door lock |
| **Compensating** | Network isolation, sandbox | Accepted risk document | Manual review | Cable lock |
| **Directive** | Mandatory AV config | AUP, classification policies | Mandatory training, procedures | Safety signage |

## Practice Questions

**Q: An organization cannot patch a critical legacy system. It isolates it on a dedicated VLAN. What type of control is this?**
Answer: **Compensating** - an alternative when the primary control (patching) is not possible.

**Q: Mandatory training explains to employees how to recognize phishing. What category and type combination applies?**
Answer: **Operational + Directive** (the training tells people what to do) and also **Operational + Preventive** (reduces the likelihood of clicking a malicious link).

**Q: An IDS fires an alert on suspicious traffic but does not block anything. What type of control is it?**
Answer: **Detective** - it detects and alerts, it does not prevent.
